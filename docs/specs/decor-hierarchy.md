# Spec: decor hierarchy (floors replace floors only)

## Goal
Painting a floor over a tile with a chair on a floor replaces only the floor; the chair stays, on top.

## Design
- Two levels: **ground** (floor and wall tiles) under **furniture** (everything else).
- Ground cells are listed in `shared/src/ground-cells.json`, generated by `tools/gen_ground_cells.py`
  from the tile sheets (`castle`, `byte_grounds_floors`, `house_inside`, `interior`, `interior_tiles`,
  `stairs`): a cell counts when >= 95% of its pixels are painted. Partial cells (trims, rugs) stay
  furniture. A catalog (not pixel reads at runtime) keeps every client and the live-edit bake
  deterministic. `shared/src/ground.ts`: `isGroundCell`, `decorLevel`.
- `stampDecor`: a ground cell replaces the ground cells on its tile at the same stack position (so
  it stays under the furniture), or goes under everything when the tile has no ground. Live
  placement (`keepGround`) puts it just above the old ground instead, so undo (which removes the
  placed cells) shows the old floor again. Furniture keeps stacking (a computer on a desk).
- Ground cells always get the floor layer and no base row; they block walking only with `all`
  (Whole piece) — the picker's default `base` is for furniture. The picker hint says so.

## Tests
`client/test/decor-hierarchy.test.ts`: floor over chair over floor, floor under existing furniture,
furniture stacking, live keep + erase order, layer/blocking rules, built-in floors recognized.
